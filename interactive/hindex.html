Where does your h-index sit?

Search for a researcher and see how their h-index compares with a random sample of people working on the same topic, especially those who started publishing around the same year. Data comes live from OpenAlex.

OpenAlex API key (free, needed for more than a couple of searches a day)

Get one at openalex.org/settings/api. It's stored only in this browser's localStorage and sent only to api.openalex.org.

OpenAlex sometimes splits one person into several profiles. Tick every profile that's you, then press Compare.

How this is calculated

  • Peers are a random sample of up to 300 OpenAlex author profiles with the chosen topic, subfield or field among their top topics. Rerunning with the same settings gives the same sample.
  • Career age = years since the first indexed work. For peers it comes from OpenAlex's per-year counts, which only go back about ten years, so anyone who started earlier goes into one "established" group on the right.
  • Your h-index is recomputed from all works in the profiles you ticked, so merged profiles are counted correctly. OpenAlex citation counts usually come out lower than Google Scholar's and closer to Scopus.
  • The trajectory is reconstructed, and the peer curve is cross-sectional (different people at each age), so treat it as a guide, not a growth chart.
